1. Why everyone is talking about GenAI

In the last two years a wave of GenAI products has appeared: ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Claude, ElevenLabs, Grok and many more. Three things are happening at once:

  • Companies are adopting AI across products, support, marketing and operations.
  • Job descriptions increasingly list AI skills as an expectation, not a bonus.
  • Students and developers build with AI: students ship projects faster, and developers integrate AI into their apps and use it to boost their own productivity.

This lecture answers the questions beginners ask first: What is GenAI? How is it different from ML and DL? What is "traditional AI" versus "modern AI"? And why learn it now?

2. What is Generative AI?

Definition. Generative AI is a technology or system that creates new content in response to a user's query or prompt, instead of returning a stored, pre-decided answer.

Two ideas matter here:

  1. New every time. Ask the same question twice and you can get two differently worded answers. The model builds the response each time rather than looking it up.
  2. Not just text. "Content" includes text, images, video, audio and programming code. That breadth is why the word generative is used.

3. AI, ML, DL and GenAI: how they relate

The lecture treats machine learning and deep learning as "traditional AI", and GenAI as modern AI. A helpful picture is a set of nested circles: AI is the broad field, ML sits inside it, deep learning sits inside ML (it uses many-layered neural networks), and generative AI is built mostly on deep learning.

4. Machine learning: the Netflix example

You watch thrillers, comedies and action films on Netflix. Over time, Netflix suggests new titles you tend to enjoy. That is machine learning at work.

How it works

  • Collect data. Netflix records what you watch: title, description, release year, genre, and also how long you watched, which signals real interest.
  • Learn from behaviour. The system learns from your past actions and choices, and from those of similar viewers.
  • Predict. It predicts what you are likely to enjoy next and shows it to you.
Key point. ML predicts or classifies using patterns in existing data. It picks from things that already exist (an existing movie) rather than creating something new. Other typical uses: spam detection, price prediction, fraud detection.

5. Deep learning: the face unlock example

When you set up face unlock, the phone captures your face from several angles. It is not memorising a single photo. It learns complex patterns: the shape of your eyes, the structure of your nose and the overall geometry of your face.

That is why unlocking still works when you wear a cap or glasses, or when the lighting changes. The model learned deep, layered features, not a flat picture.

Key point. Deep learning is a branch of ML that uses multi-layer neural networks. It excels at unstructured data such as images, speech and text. It is also the foundation GenAI is built on.

6. Traditional AI vs Generative AI

AspectTraditional AI (ML and DL)Generative AI
Main jobPredict, classify, recogniseCreate new content
OutputA label, score or recommendation from existing optionsNew text, image, video, audio or code
Same input twiceUsually the same resultOften a different result each time
ExamplesNetflix recommendations, face unlock, spam filterChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Veo, Nano Banana
InteractionMostly hidden inside an appDirectly through prompts in natural language

Traditional AI answers "which of these is it?" or "what will happen next?". Generative AI answers "make me something that fits this request."

7. GenAI model families: LLM, LIM, LVM, LAM

When you use ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude, the engine behind the scenes is a large model trained on a huge amount of data. The "L" stands for large, and the second letter says what kind of content it handles.

Details

  • LLM (Large Language Model). Trained on massive text data. It powers chatbots, summarisation, translation and code writing. The ChatGPT demo in the lecture shows this: ask "What is GenAI in short?", click regenerate, and you get a different answer to the same question.
  • LIM (Large Image Model). Generates images from a text prompt. Example from the lecture: Google Nano Banana.
  • LVM (Large Vision Model). Handles visual content and can produce images or videos from a prompt. Example: Google Veo.
  • LAM (Large Audio Model). The lecture leaves this as a question for viewers. The logical answer: it generates audio such as speech, voiceovers and music.
Note: terminology varies across the industry. "LLM" is universal. LIM, LVM and LAM are used in this course as a handy way to group models by content type, and many modern models are multimodal, meaning one model handles several types at once.

9. Quick revision and self-check

One-minute summary.
  • ML learns from past data to predict (Netflix).
  • DL uses deep neural networks to learn complex patterns (face unlock).
  • GenAI creates new content from prompts (ChatGPT, Veo).
  • LLM = text, LIM = images, LVM = vision/video, LAM = audio.
  • ML and DL are "traditional AI"; GenAI is "modern AI".
  • GenAI is becoming a core career skill.

Test yourself

1. Why is Netflix's recommendation system not generative AI?

It predicts from existing titles based on your history. It does not create new content.

2. Why does face unlock still work with glasses on?

Deep learning captured complex facial patterns (eye shape, nose structure, geometry), not just one flat image.

3. What does it mean that a GenAI answer is not "pre-decided"?

The model generates a fresh response each time, so the same question can yield different wording.

4. Which model family would you use to generate a short video from a prompt?

An LVM (large vision model), such as Google Veo.

5. What would a large audio model (LAM) generate?

Audio: speech, voiceovers, music and similar sound content.
